Codeutsav · problem statement 5

Something just happened.
What does it do to our portfolio?

A war, a tariff package, a surprise rate decision, a competitor's refinery explosion, a Category 4 hurricane in the Gulf. A portfolio manager needs three answers in minutes, and needs to trust every number in them.

Who is exposed

Which holdings the event reaches: directly, through a competitor, or through a market factor such as oil or rates.

How far could it go

What markets did in the five trading days after similar past events, weighted by how similar they were.

What to do

Value at risk, the scenario result, and a hedge plan inside position limits, with the risk before and after.

Trust

  • Nodes write evidence rows; the model writes {{E12}} placeholders, never digits.
  • The verifier rejects stray digits, unknown symbols, hedges outside limits, missing caveats.
  • A failing source degrades one node; the run continues and says what is missing.

What is real, what is simulated

Real

  • News from GDELT and Alpha Vantage; daily prices; FRED and EIA series; NHC and HURDAT2 storm tracks; EIA refinery locations.
  • A Pinecone index of 64 past events and their market reactions.
  • Claude models for planning, scoring news, hedging and writing; every number from computed evidence.
  • Replay mode cannot read anything dated after its as-of time.

Simulated

  • A fixed paper portfolio: 23 positions, $10M.
  • Hedges use ETFs in place of futures; nothing goes to a broker.
  • Hurricane replays use the best track as a perfect forecast, and say so.
  • Prices are end of day.

From an event to exposure, analogs, risk and a hedge, in under a minute, with every claim traceable.
